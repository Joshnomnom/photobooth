# 07 · What people want out of it (beyond the style)

Researched 2026-11-27. The booth's look is how it feels; this doc is about **what people walk away with** and why they use it at all.

> **Caveat on sources:** there is little independent research on photo booths. Most sources are booth vendors, app stores and trend blogs with commercial interests. Treat numbers as anecdotal and the patterns as directional.

## The outcomes people want

## Social formats (for outcome 2)

- **Instagram Story:** 1080 × 1920 (9:16). Keep content out of roughly the top and bottom 250 px, where the UI sits; this is a common guideline, not an official Meta rule. ([QuillBot](https://quillbot.com/blog/social-media/instagram-story-size/), [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/instagram-story-size), [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/instagram-story-size))
- **Feed post:** 1080 × 1350 (4:5) portrait or 1080 × 1080 square. ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/instagram-photo-size/), [icons8](https://icons8.com/blog/articles/instagram-sizes-guide-2025/amp))
- A vertical strip is ~1:3, so it sits naturally in a 9:16 Story with room for a styled background, which a raw strip PNG doesn't give you.

## What this means for us

The style is strong now. The biggest gaps are in **what you leave with**:

1. **Ready-to-post formats.** One tap for a Story (9:16), a feed post (4:5) and a phone wallpaper, each with the strip placed on a room-styled background inside safe zones, and a preview of each before saving.
2. **Live motion.** Record a short clip per pose and make a "live strip" (each frame moves) and a boomerang GIF, kept small.
3. **Occasions.** One-tap themes (Birthday, Date night, Besties, Graduation, Wedding, Holiday) that set the caption, a sticker pack and suggested paper; still fully mixable.
4. **Together modes.** Prompt sets for solo / couple / friends ("Now swap places!", "Everyone look at the camera except one person").

**Out of scope (privacy):** QR codes, galleries and "booth with friends remotely" all need uploading photos to a server, which breaks our "nothing leaves your device" promise. Web Share and downloads cover sharing without that.
