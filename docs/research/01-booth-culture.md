# 01 · Photo booth culture

What makes physical photo booths feel special, across three traditions.

## Analog (chemical) booths

**History.** Anatol Josepho's Photomaton opened in New York in 1925 and became a fixture of everyday American life. Andy Warhol famously used Times Square booths for his work. Booths declined from the 1990s, displaced first by instant cameras and then by digital booths. ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/four-for-a-quarter-7651349/), [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/photo-booth-turning-100-meet-people-keeping-alive-rcna184424))

**Today.** Only about 150–250 chemical booths survive (estimates vary). Their paper has a single remaining maker, Slavich. ([Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/articles/analog-photo-booth-disappearing-maintenance-film), [laphotoparty](https://laphotoparty.com/vintage-photo-booth-overview/))

**Why people love them:**
- **A physical keepsake.** Fans say the effort of keeping the machines alive is worth it for the object alone.
- **Privacy and control.** Behind the curtain "you're the photographer"; there's no photographer watching. ([Atlanta Magazine](https://www.atlantamagazine.com/news-culture-articles/these-atlanta-photo-booths-print-film-not-digital-strips-in-minutes/))
- **The look.** Warm, grainy, sepia-leaning strips, with no two exactly alike.
- **The wait.** Developing takes about 3–4 minutes. People hang around, laugh, and anticipate.
- **Format.** Usually 4 poses on a narrow vertical strip, with a fixed rhythm between flashes.

**Takeaways for us**
- The finished strip should feel like an object you *receive*: it comes out of a slot, develops, and you pick it up.
- The rhythm is part of it: a fixed cadence of flashes, no fiddling between shots (an optional "Film mode").
- Imperfection is a feature: frames that vary slightly, paper tone, soft edges.

## Purikura (Japan)

**The flow has two stages:**
1. **Shoot** inside the booth, with backdrops, lighting and auto-beautify.
2. **Decorate** at a touchscreen outside: stamps, pens, borders and backgrounds, laid out like a magazine page. The decorate time is limited, and depends on the queue. ([Japan Journeys](https://japanjourneys.jp/tokyo/things-to-do/the-ultimate-guide-to-purikura-japanese-photo-booths/), [Galture](https://galture.com/en/trend/purikura.html))

**Notable features.** "Big eye" enlargement (popular since a 2007 Furyu machine), skin smoothing, and props like rabbit ears and flashy glasses. ([Nippon.com](https://nippon.com/en/features/jg00029))

**Social core.** Decorating together is the fun part; "imagination is an essential part of enjoying purikura."

**Takeaways for us**
- Add a **decorate step** after the shoot: stickers, a pen, a stamped caption.
- An optional playful timer on decorating adds energy. It must not be mandatory.
- Skip face reshaping and beautify. That's off-brand for an honest, nostalgic booth, and it carries body-image concerns.

## Korean four-cut studios

Life4Cuts (인생네컷), Photoism, Haru Film and others.

- **Cheap, quick and social.** About KRW 5,000–6,000 for two prints. ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/HonoraryReporters/view?articleId=234443))
- **Themed frames** are the biggest draw: idol, artist and character frames. Photoism's "Artist Frames" put you next to a celebrity. ([enko guide](https://stay.enko.kr/blog/en/why-are-photo-booths-so-popular-in-korea-life-four-cuts-photoism-guide/))
- **Signature color looks.** Brands are recognised by their retouching and color look.
- **An analog revival.** Linked to young people's interest in film cameras and instant photos. ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/kjd-special/why-pose-smile-and-say-kimchi-to-get-on-board-with-koreas-latest-trend/10382137))
- **Scale.** Life4Cuts claims about 2.3M visits a month in Korea (company figure) and has expanded to Hong Kong and Melbourne. ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/lifestyle/fashion-beauty/article/3318250/life-4-cuts-korean-photo-booth-trend-hits-hong-kong-4-popular-ones-try))

**Takeaways for us**
- Frames and looks are the *identity*. Each of our "machines" should be instantly recognisable.
- Seasonal or limited frames are a cheap way to give people a reason to come back (later phase).
- Celebrity frames are out of scope: they need licensing and imitate real people.
